How to create accessible surveys?
---------------------------------------------
If you are working with a specific population or clinical group, you might want to adapt your survey in various ways to make it easy for your sample to interact with. 

**Resources:**

﻿`W3C Accessibility Principles <https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-principles/>`__ 

﻿`W3C How to meet WCAG (Quickref) <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/>`__

`Section 508: Fonts and typography <https://www.section508.gov/develop/fonts-typography/>`__



Add read-aloud features and ensure your text is screen-reader friendly
=============================================
:bdg-primary-line:`Sight; Accessibility for those with limited sight`
:bdg-primary-line:`Reading; Accessibility for those with reading difficulties`

Our online surveys, created, stored, and run through `Pavlovia <Pavloviapavlovia.org/dashboard?tab=4﻿>`__, are screen-reader accessible (e.g., `NVDA <https://www.nvaccess.org/>`__). To facilitate their use, avoid abbreviations or colloquialisms. If you include non-audio files in the survey, ensure they are captioned or narrated.



Use appropriate text sizes and format 
=============================================
:bdg-primary-line:`Sight; Accessibility for those with limited sight`
:bdg-primary-line:`Reading; Accessibility for those with reading difficulties`

You can control the layout of most questions in the Layout tab of the question settings (e.g., “Minimum Question Width”) after clicking on the question itself.
You can also modify its general format in the HTML markup section within the General tab. Our surveys are displayed in Arial, a Sans Serif font (i.e., without decorative strokes) to facilitate readability. If you need to change the font or any other typography detail, you can find more guidance `here <https://www.section508.gov/develop/fonts-typography/>`__.



Operable user interface
=============================================
:bdg-primary-line:`Sight; Accessibility for those with limited sight`
:bdg-primary-line:`Mobility; Accessibility for those with mobility difficulties`

Some survey question types and panels can be difficult to interact with for people with mobility difficulties, and the size of buttons cannot be changed within the layout to make this easier. Clearly indicate which question types can be completed using the keyboard (e.g., ranking questions, radio buttons, yes/no items, dropdowns, and rating scales can often be changed using the arrow keys), and avoid those that cannot be operated reliably by keyboard (such as certain checkbox formats).



Add contrast in matrix questions
=============================================
:bdg-primary-line:`Sight; Accessibility for those with limited sight`
:bdg-primary-line:`Reading; Accessibility for those with reading difficulties`

For matrix questions, you can alternate the colour of rows (Layout -> Alternate Row Colours). This can prevent line-tracking errors, reduce cognitive load and could help users who have High-Zoom on their device. 



Information must be easy to understand
=============================================
:bdg-primary-line:`Focus; Accessibility for those with focus difficulties`

Use clear, simple language.
Define complex concepts and unusual words.
Consider including a brief video or audio recording to introduce the survey instructions, alongside the written text.
